Amnesia

I was watching this documentary on a person who had a very, very short memory due to Herpes Simplex encheaphilitis, an extremely rare condition. She and people with the same condition as hers have a working memory of ~7 seconds, after which they don’t remember anything.

That can be terrifying from all aspects. What if someone tries to get advantage of you and exploit you? And what of your moments with people? What of your life?

Memories are not simply information, they are what makes a person. And what do you do when the thing that shapes your personality disappears?

Ms. Mizuta is a special case. She’s probably the only one who didn’t completely let go of herself but instead built an external scaffold to keep her memories partly alive. She built an entire external brain to store, review and understand what she’s forgotten.

She takes meticulous notes every second, on paper. A compulsive habit, but one which gives her a sense of security and allows her to store vital information. Then she reviews the notes that she took on her daily wares, triage and summarise them neatly.

But the thing is: she didn’t give up. She lives. And systems helped her do so.

I’ve had this thought before:

What if I get short term amnesia? What will make me remember myself and the world around me as I know it?

I want to create a everliving “compendium” of my ideas, worldview and the memories I’ve made so that just in case, if something happens to my memory, I’ll not be left stranded.
