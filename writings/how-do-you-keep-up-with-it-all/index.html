How do you keep up with it all?

The answer to this simple will summarise what I want to say: What would give a “smoother” experience? Running 100 Chrome tabs and then doing some video editing, or having only the video editor open?

I like to do a lot of things, especially creating things of value. I keep ambitious goals in mind (like the attached chat screenshot below) and always keep on trying to learn and experiment.

Recently one of my friends asked me how I keep up with it all, and I replied, “I don’t!”

Here’s the thing: I used to do precisely that; pile up my schedule and be busy busy busy. So many things. It didn’t work out. I was burned out, I was working at <50% efficiency, got scolded, and so on.

My modus operandi now is:

Do “something”, do it deeply and do it consistently. One, or as few things/projects, at a time.

And:

Keep up with it all by not trying to keep up.

“Keeping up with it all” is not a problem of time management, it’s of task management. It is extremely advantageous to prioritize and focus only one-two top-tier tasks. (See Eisenhower Matrix and other tools)

It’s okay to not do everything, even if you’re on a brink of changing the world. Remember, you have limited resources. Willpower, motivation and mental bandwidth are finite. It’s imperative to allocate these resources properly in order to “keep up with it all.” You’re gonna have to pick your losses, and let go.

Things will fall into their places automatically as you become consistent with your creation.

Lastly, when I work, I try to do it deeply, as in “deep work”. Cut off all internet, focus intensely on a single task, make things interesting by adding some novelty and so on. Read the summary of the book Deep Work that I wrote for more info.

I also try to make the processes a bit more efficient, for example, instead of trying to write a polished blogpost in the first go, I make a number of drafts, edit them and then let it go into the wild. Takes 10-20% more time, but this way at least I get some things done (instead of ripping my hair out and crying).

Perhaps it would be nice to accredit some of my motivations. Maybe you, the reader, would find some new inspiration as well:

  • Paul Grahams (cf. How to do great work)
  • Cal Newport (cf. Deep Work, Digital Minimalism)
  • Robin Waldun

To conclude:

  • Prioritise.
  • Engage in deep work.
  • Be consistent.
  • Keep up with it all by not trying to keep up.

Six months down the line, you’ll have a repertoire of articles, videos, podcasts, finished textbooks and what have you, to show the world.
