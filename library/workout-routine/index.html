Workout Routine

because a healthy mind follows a healthy body.

Lately, I have started looking at fitness in a different way. I realised that strength training or nutrition are not the only things that constitutes physical fitness. There are other things to consider:

  • Sleep
  • Flexibility & Mobility
  • Cardiovascular Health
  • Enjoyment and adherance

And because my schedule and environment doesn’t leave me much time to actually prepare for, start and end a full hour-long strength training workout, I am focusing on other aspects of my health.

For example, I’ve started doing flexibility and mobility workouts, more aerobic activities and such. But one thing is for certain: I must do a minimum viable workout session every single day.

Sleep & Rest#

I don’t think I have to write anything here in this section; everyone knows that sleep is important to sustain a better life.

Instead, I’ll write down several lessons I learned related to sleep and rest:

  • Maintaining about 5 sleep cycles each night (~7.5h) is okay.
  • It’s the waking time that matters. If you keep that constant, regardless of how late you sleep, the higher the chances of attaining a stability in the schedule.
  • A small dose of melatonin (about 0.3mg) helps to maintain a sleep routine.
  • Take care of caffeine doses.
  • An afternoon nap always helps me. Usually I take 5-15mins of power naps. Not more than that.
  • Meditating helps to wind down and de-stress.

Diet#

Working out and exercising is only half of the story.

Instead of following a very strict diet, I only follow this: Less Junk, More Healthy. After that, it’s only a matter of choice. You make your own diet consisting of the things that you like, yet which is healthier than what most people eat.

  • I don’t like eating out. I like to eat homemade food that my mom cooks. Sure, sometimes you go out with friends, and that’s okay.
  • Proteins are a must. I don’t really like meat that much (not a vegetarian though), so I eat eggs, cottage cheese, cereals & pulses, soy, etc.
  • I focus on maintaining my TDEE and control my portions if I am moving to and fro, from a sedentary to active lifestyle.

Strength Training#

Here’s what I used to do before I started focusing on other stuff, for 3-4 days a week. It’s a Leg-Push-Pull-Full Body split (I know, weird, right?). And because I don’t go to a gym (yet), I try to do a weighted-backpack version of the exercises that need it. For example, bicep curls are just lifting a backpack by its straps.

Exercises#

Push#

  • Push Ups x4
  • Overhead Shoulder Presses x3
  • Lateral Raises x3
  • Overhead Tricep Extensions x3

Pull#

  • Backpack Rows x4
  • Bicep Curls x3
  • Negative Chin Ups x2 (very weak on this one)
  • Bedsheet Rows x3

Legs (+ Abs)#

  • Bulgarian Split Squats x4
  • Romanian Deadlifts x3
  • Calf Raises x3
  • Hanging Knee Raises x3

Full Body#

This one’s just for “rounding out the week” and getting the muscle group frequency right.

  • Pushups x2
  • Negative Chin Ups x2
  • BSS x2
  • RDL x2
  • Lateral Raises x2
  • Bicep Curls x2
  • Hanging Knee Raises x2

For warming up and recovery, I just follow this.

Energy Management#

  • Use this electrolyte concoction: water + a tablespoon of sugar + half a tablespoon of salt + a few drops of lemon/citrus.
  • Take breaks! A minimum of 80s for every exercise is imperative.

Optimal Temperature#

So far, 18-20C is great. In any case, cooler is better.

Pre and post workout nutrition#

  • Pre-workout: 2g of instant coffee for caffeine (this has no side-effects with the lemon electrolyte mix!)
  • Post-workout: one scoop(-ish) of whey protein concentrate, mixed with room temperature water.

Misc#

  • Instead of focusing on reps and sets, focus more on tempo, form and controlled movement. That’s more important.
  • You gotta track your weight.
    • My weight (as of April, 2026) stays around 61kg
    • Compared to 66-67kg a year ago.
    • So, improvement?
  • Recovery is important. You gotta rest your muscle groups both during workouts and after.
