It started after achieving more that what I had assumed in Grade 10. I thought, what if I tried maximising with everything I got.

I thought it was noble and the moral thing to do given that I had recently found my home in philosophy and critical thinking and had the capacity to do something gread. And also because back then, my reference group consisted of teen intellectuals and rationalists that really gave me imposter syndrome.

Looking at them, and also to my long standing “Apollonian vs Dionysian” problem and my refusal to comply to the mainstream, I decided it would be nice to push my self beyond mediocrity and try doing something great.

Which was as good as a goal can be, but what fucked me was the execution and how I optimised the shit out of it.

I forgot/didn’t look for/ didn’t realise that you don’t have to juggle 20 projects at the same time to do something great. I pretended that I wasn’t made for it, which was a grave mistake. And also perhaps because of my “undiagnosed (yet) ADHD” (which I recently found out.)

The optimisation culture made me chase every little metric, track them, improve them, but don’t really think too much about the outcome. For example, I focused less on the quality of my notes and more on improving the experience of the tool. Same applied to other domains like making videos- I edited more, created less (also, cue perfectionism).

Pair that with the usage of generative AI to make my tracking and decision making “easier”, in turn making me dumber and climb the wrong branches at the same time.

I majored in the minors, as Brad Stulberg puts it, which isn’t a nice thing.
