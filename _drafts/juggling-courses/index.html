Some contextual information about me on why I am writing this post: I am a first year undergraduate student in both Cotton University (BSc in Mathematics) and IIT Guwahati (BSc in DS/AI; online), and also preparing for IAT (IISER Aptitude Test) 2027 because I couldn’t qualify in 2026.

It seems to me that I’ve enrolled into three hefty courses- one with higher, abstract math, another with high/industry level computing knowledge, and another involving deep studying in physics, chemistry, mathematics and biology. Three course with a very small intersection. This was not really what I was expecting, and sometimes it certainly feels that I am forfeiting my own ideals. But is it really that bad?

This post is part-meditation, part-documentation and part-treatise about my viewpoints on education and beyond. I hope this will make you think about some things that you didn’t think of before and get an idea of the parts that are good and that are bad.

Sequel to “How do you keep up with it all?”#

In that post I claimed that my modus operandi was:

Do “something”, do it deeply and do it consistently. One, or as few things/projects, at a time.
Keep up with it all by not trying to keep up.

And trying to work on three courses seems very much the opposite of what this says. Perhaps I shouldn’t even try to justify it, but I have an urge to say that this is not really what it seems.

Here’s what really happens:

  1. I don’t really go to college. I go 2/5 days.
  2. I have enrolled in only one “paper” in the whole trimester for the IITG course; usually people choose four.
  3. I try maximising my prep for IAT, which happens in the 5/7 days that I don’t attend college.

But as it is probably clear (by my need of trying to justify, and the fact that I am writing this), that it is a fragile and vulnerable system that fails more than it succeeds.

And that is because, really, a human has limited resources: willpower, motivation, energy, and so on. Even though I am half-assing most of the things, it still takes a lot of mental bandwidth to come up with plans, engage in social calls, etc.
