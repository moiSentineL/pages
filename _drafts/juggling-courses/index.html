Some contextual information about me on why I am writing this post: I am on a partial drop in India, preparing for IAT (IISER Aptitude Test) 2027 because I couldn’t qualify in 2026. I took admission in both Cotton University (for a BSc in Mathematics) and IIT Guwahati (for a BSc in DS/AI; online). This post is my reflection/meditation/documentation of the whole process (after being in this “situation” for about 4 months), and how it feels to do a dual-degree while preparing for a competitive exam.

So readers, I’ve enrolled myself into three hefty courses- one with higher & abstract math, another with high/industry level computing knowledge, and another involving deep studying in physics, chemistry, mathematics and biology. Three courses with negligible intersections. I wasn’t expecting this, and sometimes it certainly feels that I am forfeiting my own ideals. It does look like I am in for a good beating from all three of the academic fellows, but is it really the case? Shall we dive deep into the reasons, rules and objectives?

This post is about my viewpoints of my current sitution in education. I hope this will make you think about some things that you didn’t think of before and get an idea of the parts that are good and that are bad. Criticisms are welctome

“Couldn’t you have taken a full drop instead? Isn’t it really stressful?”#

This is one of the most common criticisms I hear about my situation, and one that gets me on the defensive, so I thought to tackle this first.

Yes, I could’ve taken a full drop. And yes, juggling three courses is stressful. But only if you consider “doing good” in all of them. That cannot happen. I am not an expert multitasker with a prodigy brain. It’s near impossible.

So why didn’t I take a full drop? And what am I doing to not stress out? Simple answer: I have specific intentions for each of the courses.

  • Reason for IAT Prep: Because I must to get into an IISER (preferably Pune) so that I can ensure 5 years of uninterrupted, competent environment, resulting in a degree which says “I am capable” from an institute of national importance.
  • Reason for College: So that I don’t have to be stuck inside my home, isolated in desolation and have a purpose to go out and socialise. I value people, I want to do something for people. So it’s prudent to be around people and be in the “real world.” [link to disillusionment]
  • Reason for the Online degree: It’s a side quest that eventually gives another social evidence of so-called “capability,” with comparitively minimal mental overhead (I can finish it in 6-7 years with no hassle).

Now, if I try to do well in my college exams, the online degree, as well as my preparation, how can I remain stress-free? I can’t! So I don’t try to do everything well- just the things that deserve it.

I half-ass the shit out the things that deserve it and replace guilt [link] with worthwhile tasks.

Note: It is really hard to remember your true intentions when most people around you are aligned to a different thing. This is why I felt FOMO while my college midterms were going on. I did study for it and they went well, but it strengthened my motive to stay away from the academic part of my college.

Sequel to “How do you keep up with it all?”#

In that post I claimed that my modus operandi was:

Do “something”, do it deeply and do it consistently. One, or as few things/projects, at a time.
Keep up with it all by not trying to keep up.

And trying to work on three courses seems very much the opposite of what this says. But this is where the intentionality comes into play.

Here’s what really happens:

  1. I don’t really go to college unless I feel suffocated in my house. I don’t really care about the academics, not because it’s subpar but because, as I’ve already mentioned above, my intention is to socialise. If I remove academic pressure and attendance from the equation (since I don’t have to perform here), it turns out to be a good thing instead of a burden. Having a place to meet a few friends and hang out, access library books, play basketball and make memories sounds like a nice plan for one of the seven days of a week.
  2. I have chosen to enroll in only one “paper” for a whole trimester for the IITG course till my IAT exam next year; usually people choose four. Since it’s a long term “side-quest” undeserving of my full, deep attention, I [ultralearn] for the weekly tests to get enough grades (80% of max is fine). I don’t have to worry about this thing for a yaer.
  3. I am maximising my energy allocation for IAT prep. As of November, though I am quite behind the schedule, I am complacent, resilient and have a plan to do well in my exam. So, the days where I don’t go to college (5-6 days), I spend studying at home. I stream on Twitch and YouTube to stay accountable and sync my tasks with my work buddy.

Before this however, around September, I was quite worried about my college attendance, the exams, proctored tests and about maintaining all of this as a whole. My perfectionism and “self-image” were holding me back. I wanted to do well because of my peers at college, novelty of being in college but when I thought about it over the Puja vacation, and realised that I had forgotten about my true intentions.

At that time, I knew the system was fragile and needed significant reinforcements (which is done, as of November 2025). I wrote this when I first started writing this essay, around September:

As it is probably clear (by my need of trying to justify my situation, and the fact that I am writing this), that it is a fragile and vulnerable system that fails more than it succeeds.

And that is because a human has limited resources: willpower, motivation, energy, and so on. Even though I am half-assing most of the things, it still takes a lot of mental bandwidth to come up with plans, engage in social calls, etc.

But I wasn’t half-assing enough, and now that I am, life is ever more blissful.

Is half-assing worth it?#

Absolutely. Half-assing is a must if you want to achieve what you actually want to. Most systems and institutes inculcate busywork (and the equivalent) to keep people under control. They want you to play their game, but remember that the house always wins. If you play by their rules, you’re always going to lose.

So half-assing things, except things that give you absolute ROI, is really good.

Life is an optimisation game. Mostly. The societal machinary is out to get you.
